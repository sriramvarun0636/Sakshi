# Data Preparation and Audit

This report documents how the SAKSHI training and evaluation data were assembled, the
leakage and label-quality problems found in the source datasets, and the decisions taken
to address them. All figures are generated by the pipeline and listed in
[tables.md](tables.md). To reproduce them:

```bash
sakshi-data download     # ~5.3 GB
sakshi-data prepare      # ~2 minutes after extraction
```

## 1. Datasets

| Dataset | Images / tweets | Labels used |
|---|---|---|
| CrisisMMD v2.0 (Alam et al., 2018) | 18,083 images, 16,058 tweets | informativeness, humanitarian category (text–image agreed), damage severity |
| Crisis Image Benchmark, AIDR-DT source (Alam et al., 2020) | 11,723 images | disaster type |
| Crisis Image Benchmark, DMD source (Mozannar et al., 2018) | 5,879 images | disaster type, damage severity |
| HumAID (Alam et al., 2021) | 41,152 tweets, text only | held-out text-robustness evaluation |

The disaster-type head is trained on the Crisis Image Benchmark (CIB) rather than on its
extension MEDIC. Only the two CIB sources that carry disaster-type labels are used
(3.2 GB, against 11 GB for MEDIC).

The result is `data/processed/manifest.parquet`: one row per image (35,685 rows), with
harmonised labels (`y_*`), the unified split (`split`), the original official splits
(`split_<task>`) and leave-one-event-out roles (`loeo_<event>`).

## 2. Split leakage in the official releases

The official CrisisMMD train/dev/test splits were drawn independently for each task:

- **3,704 images are in the test set of one task and the training set of another.**
- 7,703 images (43%) have conflicting assignments across tasks.
- 120 tweets have images on both sides of a split *within the same task*, so identical tweet text appears in training and test.
- CIB shows the same pattern: 2,549 DMD images have different official splits for disaster type and severity.

A multi-task model trained on the official files would therefore be evaluated partly on
its own training data.

**Resolution: a unified split.** Two images are linked if they belong to the same tweet,
are the same image in two datasets, or are perceptual near-duplicates (§3). Every
connected group of linked images receives the most restrictive split any of its members
was officially assigned (test > dev > train). This keeps every official test set intact,
so per-head results on the official test files remain comparable with published work. The
cost is a smaller training set:

| Official split | Official train | Retained as train |
|---|---|---|
| CrisisMMD informativeness / humanitarian | 13,608 | 8,729 (64%) |
| CrisisMMD damage severity | 2,468 | 1,090 (44%) |
| CIB disaster type | 12,724 | 11,105 (87%) |
| CIB damage severity (DMD) | 4,239 | 2,987 (70%) |

The loss of CrisisMMD severity data is offset by DMD: 4,077 severity-labelled images remain
for training. A fresh random grouped split would retain more training data but would break
comparability with every published CrisisMMD result, so it was not adopted.

## 3. Near-duplicate detection

Images are compared with a 64-bit perceptual hash (pHash). The linking threshold was set
by estimating the rate of chance collisions: the Hamming-distance distribution of one
million random image pairs was fitted on its tail and extrapolated to small distances.

| Threshold (distance ≤) | Linked pairs | Expected chance pairs | Estimated precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 3,873 | 21 | 99.5% |
| **8 (adopted)** | **4,463** | **119** | **97.3%** |
| 10 | 5,507 | 667 | 87.9% |
| 12 | 8,701 | 3,735 | 57.1% |

At the adopted threshold there are 4,463 near-duplicate pairs, plus 212 exact copies of
the same tweet image in both AIDR-DT and CrisisMMD. **2,007 of these 4,675 linked pairs
straddle the official train/test boundary.**

Two further observations:

- 604 of the pairs cross datasets, mostly because AIDR-DT includes tweets from Hurricanes Harvey, Irma and Maria.
- The largest duplicate groups are not reposted photographs but shared templates and placeholders (§6).

## 4. Disaster-type label audit

Disaster-type labels derived from the event a post belongs to would let a model identify
the event (from hashtags, place names or dates) instead of the disaster in the image. AIDR-DT
stores images in per-event folders, so we tested whether its labels had been assigned per
folder rather than per image.

- **Criterion, fixed before inspecting the data:** labels count as folder-derived if single-event folders are at least 90% one class.
- **Result:** image-weighted purity is **74.9%**. The labels are per-image annotations and are retained. The Hurricane Harvey folder, for example, contains 226 `hurricane`, 201 `flood` and 187 `not_disaster` images.
- **Independent cross-check:** 604 image pairs link CIB to CrisisMMD, whose annotators labelled the same images independently. In the 47 pairs whose CrisisMMD image is *not informative*, CIB assigns `not_disaster` in 32 (68%).
- **Semantic caveat:** every DMD image in DMD's own `non_damage` category is labelled `not_disaster`. In DMD the label therefore means "no visible damage", whereas in AIDR-DT it means "unrelated to a disaster". Disaster-type results should be reported per source.

## 5. Label harmonisation and consistency

- **Humanitarian categories:** CrisisMMD's eight raw categories are merged into the five used by the agreed-label benchmark (Ofli et al., 2020). *Injured or dead* and *missing or found people* become *affected individuals*; *vehicle damage* becomes *infrastructure and utility damage*.
- **Agreed labels:** fused tweet-level labels come from the official agreed-label files, 12,708 for informativeness and 8,079 for humanitarian category. The 109 pairs whose text and image agree only after merging are excluded, for comparability.
- **Severity gate:** all 3,526 CrisisMMD severity labels are on images whose humanitarian label passes the gate, so the hierarchical design costs no severity data. No tweet is agreed *not informative* yet assigned a humanitarian category.
- **CIB severity:** CIB's severity names (`little_or_none`, `mild`, `severe`) are mapped to CrisisMMD's.
- **Timestamps:** tweet IDs are Twitter snowflakes, which encode post time to the millisecond. All 18,083 decoded CrisisMMD timestamps fall inside their event's published collection window. AIDR-DT IDs decode the same way, and DMD file names embed post times.
- **Text and images:** 3,521 tweet texts containing double-encoded UTF-8 were repaired. No image is unreadable. 28 near-uniform images are excluded from hash matching.
- **HumAID overlap:** HumAID shares five events with CrisisMMD. No tweet IDs coincide, but 261 HumAID tweets have the same normalised text as a CrisisMMD tweet (mostly retweets). They are listed in `data/processed/humaid_exclude.csv` and must be excluded from HumAID evaluation.

## 6. Implications for provenance detection

Cross-event near-duplicates are exported to
[provenance_candidates.csv](provenance_candidates.csv) for manual verification:

- **What the matches look like:** 1,328 image pairs match across events. The median time between the two posts is **17 days**, and only one pair is more than a year apart (a 2015 Nepal earthquake image reappearing in 2018).
- **Rule matches:** 846 pairs satisfy the recycling rule (the earlier image was first posted before the later event began). About 690 of them come from four groups that are not recycled photographs:
  - an IFTTT "Image not found" placeholder
  - an "Email this" icon
  - 91 National Hurricane Center forecast-cone maps, different storms sharing one template
  - a broadcaster logo
- **What remains:** about 150 pairs in 54 groups, mainly photographs shared between concurrent 2017 disasters, such as Harvey images reposted during Maria.

Consequences:

1. **The main quantitative evaluation of provenance detection must be the controlled injection experiment.** Recycling in these datasets is predominantly *misattribution between overlapping events*; years-old images resurfacing are rare.
2. **Template and placeholder graphics are the dominant false-positive class.** The detector needs an explicit filter for them, and the evaluation must include them as hard negatives.
3. **These groups do not add label noise for classification.** Annotators labelled the placeholders consistently as *not informative* and the forecast maps as *informative*.

## 7. Limitations

- The provenance rule uses each event's collection start date as its onset. This is conservative, since actual onsets were a few days earlier.
- Near-duplicate linking is limited to pHash; re-cropped or heavily edited copies may remain unlinked. The embedding-based matcher planned for provenance detection could serve as a second deduplication pass.
- HumAID uses ten humanitarian categories that do not map one-to-one onto CrisisMMD's five, so a mapping must be defined before the text-robustness evaluation.
